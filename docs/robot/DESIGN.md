# RPRR-400 engineering plan — revision A

2026-11-14. Design owner: cadder. This is an analytical concept and a specification for CAD; it is not a released manufacturing package or a tested robot. Onshape remains authoritative once a native model is verified. All sizes below are mm unless stated otherwise.

## Interpretation and operating envelope

Four actuated axes, in order: base yaw Rz, vertical carriage translation Pz, elbow yaw Rz, wrist yaw Rz. This is a SCARA-like arm; **elbow and wrist rotate about vertical axes, not horizontal pitch/roll axes**. The brief does not specify their axis directions. This choice gives planar positioning plus vertical travel and tool yaw while avoiding gravity torque at the rotary motors. It cannot tilt a tool. Payload is 200 g, additional to a budgeted 100 g tool and all arm components.

Base-to-elbow distance 220; elbow-to-wrist 160; tool point 20 radially from wrist. Fully aligned reach is 400 from the base rotation axis to the tool point. Wrist angle zero aligns the offset with link 2. Maximum reach is a kinematic singularity: use it as a slowly approached boundary, not a high-speed through-path. Continuous productive side coverage is the line x=300, y=-200…+200 at constant height, not an assertion that every point in a 400×400 square is accessible. Coverage has been checked with the 20 mm tool offset included. At the endpoints, (base, elbow, wrist) are (-56.720°,51.595°,0°) and (10.660°,51.595°,0°); at the center they are (-36.580°,83.329°,0°). Use the positive-elbow branch throughout.

Limits: base ±120°, elbow 0…140°, wrist ±150°, carriage travel 200. The elbow's zero stop must allow the true 400 reach, with software slowing within 5° of straight. Ordinary operation: each rotary joint ≤0.5 rad/s and ≤0.5 rad/s²; Z ≤5 mm/s and ≤100 mm/s². Jerk-limited trajectories and homing switches on all axes. Base and wrist are limited-turn axes with service loops, not slip-ring axes. No claimed absolute accuracy; initial acceptance targets are ≤2 mm loaded static tip droop, ≤0.5 mm bidirectional repeatability after calibration, and no missed steps over the acceptance trajectory.

## Mechanical arrangement and interfaces

The reproducible `kinematics.py` check samples the side sweep every 1 mm at both lift endpoints (802 configurations). Forward/inverse position error is below 1e-10 mm; base extrema are -56.944 degrees and 10.660 degrees, so checking only endpoints misses the most negative base angle. The sweep stays away from the straight-arm singularity. This verifies positions and joint limits, not collision clearance, fixed tool yaw, trajectory speed or acceleration. Evidence: `artifacts/robot/workspace-validation.json`.

Mount a 200×200×10 aluminum ground plate to a rigid bench with four M8 bolts on a 160 square; never rely on an unballasted desktop base. Base shaft is 12 mm steel in two NSK 6001 bearings with centers 60 apart. An axial shoulder, spacer and clamping collar retain the rotating tower; the fixed housing retains both outer rings with removable covers. Bearing fits must be assigned from vendor fit guidance during detail design, not printed as loose nominal bores. Rotating mast: four 450-long HFS5-2020 posts at corners of an 80×80 center square, tied with machined top/bottom plates and side gussets. Four independent posts are credited in conservative bending calculations; no composite stiffness credit is taken for the connecting plates.

Two 350-long MGN12 rails are mounted vertically on a flat, machined 6 mm aluminum rail backing plate, bolted to the mast posts. Rail center spacing 80; two MGN12C blocks per rail, block centers 100 vertically apart. A 120×140×6 carriage plate spans all four blocks. Rails are not mounted directly on an unfinished extrusion face. Carriage travel 200 leaves nominal 150 for the block group and end clearances; exact rail-end/hard-stop locations must be established from the actual block dimensions. Locate rails on the forward face of the mast and the horizontal arm above the rail carriage so link 1 clears the tower.

Link 1 uses one genuine 2020 extrusion with 220 joint-center spacing, link 2 one with 160 spacing; physical cut lengths are shorter than joint-center spacing and must be set by bearing brackets, not silently equated to those lengths. Use paired 6 mm aluminum gusset plates with at least two longitudinally spaced M5 slot nuts per interface. Link 1 and link 2 are vertically staggered by 55 at their centerlines; this permits the distal link to pass above the proximal one. Elbow bearing centers 35 apart, wrist centers 25 apart. Both use two 6001 bearings and 12 mm steel shafts. Elbow motor sits on link 1, its center about 150 from base; wrist motor sits on link 2, its center about 90 from elbow. Their 390 g masses are included. Use shaft-clamping output pulley hubs; do not transmit joint torque through single grub screws alone.

Preliminary canonical bearing carrier: 60×60×12, central Ø28 seat, four Ø5.5 mounting holes on a 44 square, two Ø3.3 cover-retention clearance holes (locations to be collision-checked). Separate cover has Ø24 clearance and matching retention holes. NEMA17 brackets use 31×31 M3 mounting pattern and ≥Ø22.5 pilot relief, checked against the selected motor drawing before release; bracket thickness/bolt length must not bottom bolts in the motor. M5 connections use washers and ≥8 mm usable nut engagement; all machined hole tolerances, shaft fits, screw grades and tightening torques remain drawing-release items.

Maintain ≥5 hard-part clearance through the admitted operating envelope, ≥10 around cable loops, guarded pulley nip points and physical stops outside software limits. This is a clearance requirement, **not a completed sweep**. A full 3D sweep with actual motor/pulley envelopes is required before manufacturing. The coverage branch keeps elbow below 84°, reducing folded-arm collision risk; other joint-limit corners may require a restricted collision map.

The first link is rigidly attached to the lift carriage; there is no fifth shoulder joint. The 220 distance is measured from the virtual base yaw axis inside the tower to the elbow. The actual link extrusion starts forward of the tower, provisionally x=100, and ends at the elbow bracket. This standoff is included in the 220 reach dimension. The 0.110 kg link budget conservatively counts a full 220 extrusion before trimming; the bracket budget must accommodate the standoff. The detailed CAD must establish this stackup without a link passing through the mast.

## Lift and behavior without power

Use an igus single-start TR10×2 screw/nut system, 350 nominal screw length subject to end-machining layout, with a separate normally unloaded safety nut that catches a worn primary nut. A 20:20 GT2 belt allows a standard single-shaft NEMA17 to drive the screw while a spring-applied SWB-01 brake acts **on the screw-side shaft**, independently of the belt. The 5 mm brake hub requires a supported machined shaft extension/coupler; the brake does not carry axial load. Support the screw with a dedicated thrust-bearing pair at the fixed end and a floating radial bearing at the other. Nut carrier must allow alignment adjustment without transmitting guide moments into the screw. Do not rely on motor bearings for screw thrust.

At 2 mm lead and 150 rpm, Z speed is 5 mm/s; a 200 mm lift takes at least 40 s. With conservative 20% screw efficiency, 90% belt efficiency and 5 N friction allowance, required motor torque is 0.0678 N·m for the 3.3625 kg design moving mass. Faster lift is possible but is not part of this acceptance envelope. Single-start trapezoidal units are described by igus as self-locking, but the brake is retained because real lubrication, vibration and wear must be considered. Ideal frictionless gravity backdrive torque is 0.0105 N·m; nominal 0.25 N·m brake holding rating provides 23.8× static ratio. This ratio does not prove emergency stopping distance. Power-up sequence: establish motor current, then release brake; stop sequence: decelerate, engage brake, then remove current. Verify actual brake response and catch distance with a tethered load test. A broken screw or failed nut attachment is not covered by the brake; safety nut and bottom energy-absorbing stop remain required. No people may be under the load.

## Calculations and evidence

Run `.venv/Scripts/python.exe docs/robot/calculate.py`. The script writes `artifacts/robot/calculations.json` and includes every lumped mass and its location. Nominal arm/tool/payload mass is 2.09 kg; carriage adds 0.60 kg. Apply 25% mass reserve, including payload, before sizing. Full-reach gravity moment is 6.498 N·m. Rotary acceleration bounds use the triangle inequality on tangential and centripetal acceleration of every serial-chain segment, including simultaneous joint motion; then apply a further 2× torque factor. A separate 0.08 kg·m² rotating mast/slide inertia allowance is added at the base. Friction allowances are 0.05/0.03/0.01 N·m at base/elbow/wrist. These are explicit design estimates to be measured, not manufacturer data.

Selected motor for all axes: STEPPERONLINE 17HS19-2004S1, 42×42×48, 390 g, 2 A/phase, 3 mH. Its manufacturer **dynamic pull-out curve at 24 V, 2 A, half-step** is about 0.30 N·m near 60 rpm, ~0.42 N·m near 150–300 rpm, and ~0.23 N·m at 750 rpm. Calculations use a conservative 0.25 N·m design allowance rather than the 0.59 N·m holding rating. The selected slow rotary operating points (14–24 motor rpm) are below the published plotted range; **0.25 N·m there is an engineering allowance, not measured curve data**. Hardware torque/resonance testing at the actual driver/current/microstep setting is a release gate. Use 24 V current-regulated drivers, set the current convention correctly (peak versus RMS), and verify hot-motor performance. Do not claim the plotted half-step performance for another driver setting without testing.

Base 20:100 reduction, elbow 20:80, wrist 20:60; transmission efficiency 90%. Script outputs factored motor torque, remaining margin relative to 0.25 N·m, belt pitch lengths, minimum wrap and belt tension differences. Pulley center distances are initially 80/70/65 and must be adjusted to selected catalog belt lengths using ±5 mm slotted motor mounts. Use matching 2 mm-pitch GT2/2MR tooth profiles and 9 mm belts; generic “GT2” labeling is not proof of compatible geometry. All small pulleys engage more than six teeth. The calculated design tangential belt loads are inputs to final Gates selection, not a fabricated vendor belt load rating. Select belt/pulley catalog part numbers and verify their low-speed tooth-load ratings and bearing preload before release. No belt strength margin is claimed yet.

Rail pitch couple per block is 32.49 N from gravity moment/(2×0.100 m); add axial lift load, yaw/tangential loading and mounting error during detail validation. MGN12C published static capacity is 3920 N per block, far above these idealized loads; rail alignment and backing stiffness dominate. Base bearing gravity-moment couple is 108.3 N, conservative elbow bound 185.7 N before belt pretension. Specify total conservative radial bearing reaction ≤300 N including pretension, giving 7.9× against NSK 6001 static rating 2370 N. This is not a complete ISO life/combined-load calculation. Separately confirm axial load and retention capacity.

MISUMI HFS5-2020 properties: 0.5 kg/m and Ixx=Iyy=7420 mm⁴. E=69 GPa is an assumed aluminum elastic modulus. Euler–Bernoulli straight-arm approximation gives 0.539 mm tip bending; the four-post mast contributes 0.571 mm from rotation at the tip and 0.321 mm translation at its top (450 mm exposed height), total structural estimate 1.431 mm. Joint compliance, rail deflection and belt elasticity are excluded, leaving only ~0.57 mm to meet the 2 mm droop target. If measurement or detailed stiffness analysis exceeds this, increase link sections using paired 2020s and revise masses before release. Maximum simple bending stress is 8.76 MPa in a single 2020 at the full moment; this says nothing about local fastener/slot failure. The base is bench-bolted; calculate actual anchor loads after shaft and ground-plate positions are fixed.

## CAD sequence and measurable acceptance

1. Review calculations and resolve component-profile interfaces. Build a **small native-feature-source bearing carrier fixture** locally, including centered bore and scoped REMOVE holes. OpenCascade must prove one valid solid, expected analytical volume, bounds and through holes before any browser write.
2. Prove the same fixture in a new Onshape Part Studio: independent saved definitions, native editable sketch constraints, exact target-body binding, volume/bounds, ordinary dimension change and restoration. Preserve car/plate/sample content. Tester is sole live owner until root transfers it.
3. Build canonical reusable parts with genuine open T-slot extrusion profiles derived from manufacturer drawings. Purchased bearings/motors/rails/screw may use clearly named editable interface envelopes that preserve fit dimensions; do not represent a smooth cylinder as a fabricated toothed pulley/thread, or claim the envelopes are manufacturing geometry. No imported STEP/mesh and no opaque custom feature.
4. Source-bound assembly insertion and fixed connections, then three revolutes and one slider with limits. Establish occurrence/source identity. Sweep the side-coverage trajectory at minimum/maximum Z and joint-limit corners with actual bounding/interface geometry. Verify ≥5 mm hard-part clearance and ≥10 cable clearance; resolve exceptions through explicit travel restrictions or geometry changes.
5. Independent OpenCascade and Onshape comparison: expected solid count per part, volume within 0.1%, bounds within 0.1 mm; edit link length or plate thickness and restore with stable identities. Recompute all affected loads and clearance tests after dimension changes.
6. Physical release gates: measured mass within reserve; 200 g payload plus tool; loaded tip droop ≤2 mm; repeatability ≤0.5 mm; no missed steps over 100 full side sweeps; tethered power-loss test with acceptable measured stopping distance; verified belt catalog load selection, bearing fits, nut safety catch, thermal behavior and guards. Analytical feasibility is conditional until these gates pass.
